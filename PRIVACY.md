# Triage and your privacy

This is what Triage 0.4 says about itself, word for word as it is in the app under Settings, Privacy and data. "I" is Triage.

**Read and sorted on this phone.** Whatever you let me look at, I read on this phone and nowhere else: your calendar, contacts, health, steps, where you are, the apps you use, your mail and your messages. There's no Triage server, so none of it reaches the people who made me. None of it is shared, with anyone.

## What I read

- The newest 30 messages in the inbox of each mailbox you add: who they're from, the subject, a few header lines and the first 300 characters. I read the whole of a message when you open it or ask for a reply to it, and for the few that need you or look as if they want something from you, so the AI can say what each one wants. I fetch a file attached to a message only when you tap it.
- To tick off mail you've already answered or passed on, whenever I read your mail and when you come back to Triage, I check whether your mail provider has marked a message as answered, and I look at the newest 25 messages in each mailbox's Sent folder. Of those I take only which message each one answers, who it was sent to, including anyone you blind-copied, when it was sent, whether it was sent automatically, whether a file went with it, and its subject, from the last 7 days. The subject only tells me whether it was a forward. I don't save any of it, and I never look at what it says.
- When I look back through your phone, and once for each mailbox you add: up to a year of its mail, filed away or not, and its Sent folder: the newest 3,000, 3,000 more from the people you write to most, and 1,500 you sent, headers only. I note who each is from or to, when, its subject and whether you answered, to learn who matters to you and what arrives on a schedule, like a bill each month. Never what a message says. Once a week the AI on your phone reads the subjects of your longest conversations together, with whom and when, to find the matters they serve, like a visa application; the names it finds stay on your phone. After that, every few hours, I read what you wrote in your newest 25 sent messages for anything you promised someone, and keep the promise in a line, not your words. I also count, by sender, the messages you leave unread for three days. After five in a row I ask, once, whether to put that sender's mail away. If you say yes and let me tidy your mailbox, I move their mail to its archive; I never delete it.
- If you turn on "Notice when I'm on the move": I learn whether you're still, walking, running, cycling or in a vehicle, which Android tells me when it changes. If you also allow nearby devices, I notice when your car's Bluetooth connects: only that it's a car kit, never its name.
- If you turn on "Notice the places I stop": I take the locations other apps already asked for, which costs no battery, and ask where the phone is myself only when you stop and none is fresh. I also note the name of the wifi you're on at each place, so being on it again tells me where you are without asking.
- If you turn on "Know how I slept": I read how long you slept last night from Health Connect, once a day, your heart rate of the last few minutes, and whether a workout is being recorded. If you allow it, I read these with Triage closed too.
- If you turn on "Notice what I listen to", and chats are on: I see which app is playing, the artist, and when it starts and stops. Android tells me through the same access.
- If you turn on "Your contacts": I look up whoever wrote to you, and the others an email you open was sent to, in your contacts, by their email address or their name, for their photo and to know whether they're in your contacts. I read nothing else of a contact, and I never read your contacts as a list.
- If you turn on "Show today's plans": I read what's left of today in your calendar, each event's name and its times, and I show it on your list. I also read who's at your meetings, from a month back to two weeks ahead, except those with more than 12 guests: each guest's name and email address, and the meeting's name, which I keep on their card.
- If you turn on "Your other apps", and chats are on: I read the alerts your other apps post, their title and what they say, except the ones you switch off one by one. I keep the newest from each app among your chats, for as long as a chat is kept. For an app you tell me to, I also take down its own notification, and nothing else of it.
- If you allow Usage access: I see which app is in front and when, which Android keeps for about four weeks. I use it to tell when you're working, and to take an alert or a chat off your list once you've opened the app it came from. I never see what you do inside an app.
- If you allow it, I read message notifications from your chat apps and your texting app, and take a copy of a photo sent in one. I never read calls. I tick off a chat when the newest line in its notification is your own, which is when you answer from the notification or from Triage. I can't see what you write inside a chat app, so a chat you answer there leaves the list without a tick.
- If you allow it, I read the text messages on this phone that you haven't opened yet, straight from the phone's own messages: who sent each one, what it says, when it came, whether it was sent to other people too, and the first picture in a picture message. With one a person sent I also read the last 25 messages of its conversation from the last 7 days, theirs and yours, so you and the AI on this phone can see what came before. Of your other texts from the last 7 days, I look only at when each came and whether you've opened or answered it, never what it says. I tick off a text you've answered. I can't send a text by myself, or change or delete one. A reply you write in Triage is handed to your texting app, which sends it when you tap Send.
- If you allow it, I read your calendar: only whether you're busy right now. Who's at an event, and what it's called, only if you turn on "Show today's plans".
- While "Tell me when something needs me" is on, I look at your newest mail about twice an hour while Triage is closed, so I can tell you what's arrived. And, so I can write a reply the way you write, I read the newest 8 messages in the Sent folder of your first mailbox, about once a day, and your own lines in the chat notifications above.

## What stays on this phone

- I keep your mailbox addresses, and their passwords or sign-ins, encrypted with a key that never leaves this phone.
- I also keep the mail on screen as its sender, its subject and its first lines, how I sorted each message, what the AI said each one wants with the names of its files and links, the reminders you set, what you marked Done or Junk, how long you spend reading each sender and how you deal with them at each time of day.
- If you turn them on under "Your day", I keep how you were last moving, up to 30 places you've stopped at, each as a point on the map and how often you stop there, never a name or an address, the wifi name at each, and how long you slept last night. I delete each when you turn its switch off. I also keep a diary of the last 60 days: a line each time any of these changes, saying which of your places you were at, how you were moving, whether the car or headphones were connected and whether your calendar had you busy. It holds no messages and no route. Your modes are found in it, and I remember how you deal with each sender in each mode.
- I keep anything you add under "What's on your mind", and the names of anything you tell me not to show there.
- For each matter - yours, or one you've named yourself - I keep a short line for each thing that happened with it: what it was, what you did about it, and when you wrote back, never the message itself or what you wrote. I keep the newest 50 for each.
- For each person who writes to you, I keep one card: their name, how they reach you (their email address, or their name in a chat app), whether they're in your contacts, when I first saw them and last heard from them, how many messages came from them and where, how many you answered and about how long you took, how many messages you've written to their address, and the name and phone numbers they sign their mail with, and when I last offered to add them to your contacts and whether you said not now. I also make a card for people you haven't heard from who were on a message you got or at one of your meetings: their name and address, and the subject of the message or the name of the meeting, the newest five. Where I can, I read the signatures of their latest messages, archived ones too, for their job, their firm and where they are, and I keep only those. Never what a message said. I keep the newest 400 cards.
- If you turn on "Notice what I listen to", I keep your last 200 times of listening, each as the day, the time, how long, the app and the artist. I delete them when you turn it off.
- I keep a mark for each message you've answered or marked Done, with when and how I know. A mark says nothing of who sent a message or what it said. What you've dealt with is shown under Done until the end of the next day at the latest; I keep the newest 300 marks so that it isn't shown as needing you again.
- While a message is open, I hold its whole text in memory, to show you and so the AI on this phone can answer what you ask about it. Your questions about a message, the AI's answers, and a reply it drafts for you with the text it was written from stay in memory too, until you mark the message Done, close Triage, or have been out of it for 30 minutes. None of that is written to the phone's storage.
- I keep files you open from your mail for up to 7 days.
- If you allow chats, I keep the newest message of up to 50 chats, for up to 7 days, with a photo that came with one, and the names of the chat apps they came from. One you've answered stays, marked as answered, until the end of the next day, even if you open it in its own app. Then I delete it, unless you've put it back on your list.
- If you allow texts, I keep the newest unread text from each sender, with the number it came from, among those 50 chats for up to 7 days. If I recognise a text as a sign-in code, I keep it for 1 day at most. I delete a text sooner when you read it in your texting app. Those earlier messages I read from your phone each time and don't keep. One you've answered stays, marked as answered, until the end of the next day, even if you open it in its own app. Then I delete it, unless you've put it back on your list. If you answer a text within 10 minutes of opening it, it comes back ticked. I also keep a mark for each text already seen, so I don't show one twice: a mark says nothing of who sent a text or what it said.
- While "Tell me when something needs me" is on, I keep up to 12 short examples of your own writing, from your sent mail, your chat lines and the replies you send from Triage or from a notification, and a mark for each message I've told you about. For each one I also note who it was to, and I only use it when you reply to that same person, so a letter to one person is never used to write to another. I delete the examples when you turn it off, and with the mailbox or the chat app they came from.
- When you remove a mailbox, I delete its password, its mail as I kept it, its reminders, what I sorted and the AI said about its mail, and the files opened from it. Delete my data, at the end of Settings, deletes the messages and what I made of them, or all of it.
- None of it goes into backups or gets copied to a new phone.
- Uninstalling Triage removes all of it as well.

## Where I connect

- Your mail provider's servers: I use them to read your mail, to send the replies you approve and, only if you allow it, to mark mail you've finished with as read and move mail you delete, report as spam or unsubscribe from to its bin or spam folder, a few seconds after you ask.
- A one-time lookup of your mail domain (not your address) at Thunderbird's public directory, autoconfig.thunderbird.net, when I don't already know your provider.
- Microsoft's sign-in, for Outlook mailboxes.
- huggingface.co, only if you download Gemma 4.
- The place Triage is published, when you open it: I ask whether there is a newer version, and fetch it if you say so. Nothing about you is sent.
- Google, the first time a message comes in a language I haven't translated before: your phone downloads that language, once, so I can translate it here. The message itself is never sent.
- A newsletter's own unsubscribe address, only when you tap Unsubscribe: a request to stop, and nothing else about you.
- Only if you allow Vanity: what needs you, who it's from, what it's about, what to do, why and by when, to the Vanity app on this phone, which passes it to your own Vanity server. Never whole messages, and never your passwords.
- If you turn on "Notice the places I stop", your phone's own location service works out where it is. With Google Location Accuracy on in your phone's settings, that service asks Google, as it does for any app. I don't send where you are to anyone.
- Diagnostics that Google's on-device AI and translation libraries send to Google: device and app information, identifiers and performance figures. It never includes your mail or messages.

## Worth knowing

- Where the phone has Android's own on-device AI, Android may download its model over wi-fi.
- I translate messages that aren't in your phone's language on this phone. The first time a language comes up, your phone downloads it from Google, once. Your messages are never sent to be translated.
- I write replies and answers to your questions with an AI on this phone, and it can be wrong. I send nothing until you've read it and tapped Send.
- My notifications name the sender and show the first lines of the message. On a locked phone they show only how much it matters, unless you've set the phone to show everything on its lock screen.
- A reply you send to a chat from Triage or from a notification is handed to that chat's own app on this phone, which sends it as yours. Where I can't send into a chat, I copy your reply instead, for you to paste there, and it stays on the phone's clipboard until you copy something else.
- I'm for adults, 18 and over.
